# Sentinel: rehearsal playbook

Read this twice. Then rehearse the script out loud three times with a timer. The third run is the one that counts.

## The thesis (one sentence)

Detection is solved; Sentinel is the unsolved last mile: a public signal, in any language, turned into *our* exposure, a decision, and the right desk, with a quote behind every sentence.

## Three numbers to memorize

1. **18 h / ~8 days (Foshan) · 18 h before landfall (Otis).** Foshan replay: Sentinel fires 18 hours before the first English headline, about eight days (183 h) before Bloomberg. Strict rule. Reproducible.
2. **$1.47B.** Chubb's pre-tax loss from the LA wildfires in one quarter (Q1 2025). The fire was public from hour one.
3. **$3.48B · 4 decisions.** What's on the live screen today. Polo: 62 sites, $1.52B, Cat 5 issued by NHC, none in the core, action "restrict new binding, Baja California Sur."

Pocket numbers: 478 cases (the notice), 51 h (first Chinese signal), 3,728 insured lives (Ebola, WHO "very high"), ~$700M (Helene + Milton), $137B (2024 global insured cat).

## Say it so a non-insurance judge follows (use these the first time each word comes up)

- **Chubb**: the largest publicly traded property & casualty insurer in the world. They insure buildings, cargo, travellers, executives.
- **Dataminr / BlueDot**: alert services that scan news and social media and tell you something happened. They detect; they don't say what it means for you.
- **Accumulation manager / cat desk**: the team that tracks how much of the company's risk is piled up in one place, and watches storms and quakes against it.
- **Book**: the insurer's portfolio of policies. "Our book" = everything we've insured.
- **TIV**: total insured value, the dollar amount at risk.
- **Binding**: agreeing to insure something. "Restrict new binding" = stop writing new policies in the danger zone until the storm passes.
- **A&H**: accident & health insurance, counted in insured lives, not dollars.
- **Reserves**: money set aside for claims. We never tell them to change reserves before a storm hits.

## Top 8 Q&A (two sentences each, then stop; offer to show it on screen)

1. **How do you stop the LLM from hallucinating?**
Severity is copied from NHC, USGS PAGER, GDACS or WHO, never generated, and every sentence in a brief must carry a verbatim quote that a plain-code Verifier string-matches against the fetched source. Anything without a matching quote is dropped, and the score is a transparent formula with its breakdown on screen.

2. **What about false positives and alert fatigue?**
One feed alone only gets "watch"; an alert needs at least two independent publishers, and the Router only pages a desk when the event touches the book or the authority's severity crosses a threshold. Duplicates are suppressed across cycles and it escalates only when severity or exposure goes up.

3. **Why not just buy Dataminr, BlueDot or Moody's RMS?**
They detect and model hazard; we're the last mile none of them sells: detection, to your exposure, to a decision in underwriter vocabulary, routed to the right person with a quote behind every sentence. Their feeds plug in as extra Fetch agents; we complement them.

4. **Hurricane-force winds reach 50–60 km. How many of those 62 sites are really at risk?**
The 350 km band is a screening buffer around the NHC forecast track, the accumulation-check universe, and we separately report sites and TIV inside the hurricane-force core, which is zero right now. The buffer drives binding restrictions; the core drives claims staging; in a pilot we'd swap in Chubb's own footprint or cat-model event set.

5. **Your backtest is n = 1 and the strict alert fires on an English Hong Kong letter. Where's the multilingual edge?**
The edge is the 51 hours when the event existed only in Chinese and was already on Sentinel's watch list; the strict rule deliberately waits for an authority or a second language before paging a desk, trading lead time for fewer false alarms. Yes, n = 1, fully sourced; the pilot's second deliverable is a backtest across a season of Chubb's own events so lead time becomes a distribution.

6. **How is Chubb's exposure data secured?**
It never leaves Chubb: Sentinel runs in Chubb's own cloud account, the overlay is a local spatial join, and the model sees only event text and aggregates like "62 sites, $1.52B", never a policyholder. LLM calls can go through Chubb's approved enterprise endpoint.

7. **What does it cost to run?**
Most stages are deterministic code; Claude is called only for events that pass the corroboration gate, responses are cached by evidence hash, and a full cycle is under a dollar. Steady state is a few dollars a day at a ten-minute cadence, roughly one analyst hour a month.

8. **What's real and what's synthetic?**
Real: every feed, event, quote, URL, severity level, the run log and the backtest timestamps. Synthetic: the insurance book behind the exposure numbers, because we don't have Chubb's data and won't pretend to; swapping in the real exposure file is the pilot's first deliverable.

Backups in QA.md: licensing (free public feeds; quotes and URLs stored, not articles), scale (stateless agents, parallel by event), multilingual (en/es/fr/pt/hi/zh; quotes verified in the original language), go-to-market (land with one desk, expand to A&H and Claims, annual platform licence).

## Words to never say

- **"footprint"** for our buffer. Say *screening buffer* and *hurricane-force core*. "Footprint" means a cat-model product to this audience and invites the 50 km question on bad terms.
- **"strengthen reserves" / "touch reserves" / "reserving decision."** The action is *restrict new binding* or *pre-stage adjusters*. Reserves are the CFO's word and a regulatory minefield.
- **"AI decides" / "the AI recommends."** Say *Sentinel surfaces a decision for the desk*, *severity is copied from NHC*, *code verifies*. The human owns the decision.
- **"predicts."** Sentinel corroborates and overlays; it does not forecast. NHC forecasts.
- **"real Chubb data" / "Chubb's book."** It's a *synthetic book*. Say "synthetic" out loud, at least once, unprompted.
- **"hallucination-free."** Say *no quote, no claim* and let them draw the conclusion.
- **"nine days before Bloomberg."** The headline number is *about eight days (183 h) before Bloomberg* from the strict alert; nine days is notice-to-Bloomberg on slide 1 and is correct only in that framing.
- **"we scraped."** Say *public feeds* and *headlines and links*.
