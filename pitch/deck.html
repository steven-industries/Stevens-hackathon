Why an underwriter can trust it

Three rules.
The model never gets to break them.

1
Severity is copied, never generated.
Cat 5 comes from NHC. Risk "very high" comes from WHO. PAGER from USGS. The language model reads it; it is not allowed to write it.
2
No quote, no claim.
Every sentence in a brief carries a verbatim quote. A Verifier written in plain code string-matches it against the fetched source. Not found, dropped.
3
The score shows its math.
A transparent formula with the breakdown on screen, and a required field called "What we don't know yet".

How we built it

Seven public feeds. One orchestrator. Nine agents.

What we watch, every ten minutes
NHC
US National Hurricane Center. Official storm category, wind speed and forecast track.
GDACS
UN and EU disaster alert system. Colour-coded alerts for storms, floods, quakes and fires worldwide.
USGS
US Geological Survey. Earthquake magnitude and a PAGER estimate of people affected.
WHO DON
World Health Organization outbreak news. Case counts and the WHO's own risk rating.
ECDC
Europe's disease control agency. Weekly threat reports.
NASA EONET
Satellite-tracked natural events: wildfires, volcanoes, severe storms.
Local news
Google News in English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Hindi and Chinese. Weak signals before the wires.
How it runs
  • One Python orchestrator runs a cycle every ten minutes and logs every agent step, so any alert can be replayed.
  • Claude clusters signals, writes the briefs and the notifications. Plain code fetches, verifies every quote, computes exposure on a map, scores and routes.
  • Exposure is a spatial join: the storm track and its buffer against the insurer's sites and insured lives. Today's book is synthetic; the pilot uses Chubb's.
  • Resilient by design. Feeds cache to disk, the demo runs offline, and if the model is down every stage has a rule-based fallback.
  • Dashboard is a single static page. No build, no database, deploys anywhere.

Why now, why Chubb

It sits on top of feeds Chubb already buys.

"Investing in data and AI to be faster in underwriting and claims."
Chubb, 2025 Letter to Shareholders (paraphrased)

A language model can now read a Chinese health-bureau bulletin and an NHC advisory in the same pass, for cents. Two years ago that was a research project. Today it is a ten-minute cron job.

  • Complements, doesn't replace. Alert services (Dataminr) and catastrophe models (Moody's RMS) detect and estimate. Their feeds plug straight in under Chubb's existing contracts.
  • Exposure never leaves. Runs in Chubb's own cloud. The model sees event text and aggregates ("62 sites, $1.52B"), never a policyholder.
  • Built for the 6 a.m. desk. The accumulation manager (the person who tracks how much of the company's risk is piled up in one place), the head of accident & health, Claims and the investment chief each get their own view of the same event.
  • Honest about what's synthetic. Feeds, events, quotes, severity: real. The book: synthetic, until the pilot.
