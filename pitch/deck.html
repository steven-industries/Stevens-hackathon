Why an underwriter can trust it

Three rules.
The model never gets to break them.

1.
Severity is copied, never generated.
Cat 5 comes from NHC. Risk "very high" comes from WHO. PAGER from USGS. The language model reads it; it is not allowed to write it.
2.
No quote, no claim.
Every sentence in a brief carries a verbatim quote. A Verifier written in plain code string-matches it against the fetched source. Not found, dropped.
3.
The score shows its math.
A transparent formula with the breakdown on screen, and a required field called What we don't know yet.

Why now, why Chubb

It sits on top of feeds Chubb already buys.

Investing in data and AI to be faster in underwriting and claims.
Chubb, 2025 Letter to Shareholders (paraphrased)

A language model can now read a Chinese health-bureau bulletin and an NHC advisory in the same pass, for cents. Two years ago that was a research project. Today it is a ten-minute cron job.

  • Complements, doesn't replace. Dataminr and Moody's RMS detect and model. Their feeds plug in as extra Fetch agents under Chubb's existing contracts.
  • Exposure never leaves. Runs in Chubb's own cloud. The model sees event text and aggregates ("62 sites, $1.52B"), never a policyholder.
  • Built for the 6 a.m. desk. The accumulation manager, the head of A&H, Claims and the CIO each get their own lens on the same event.
  • Honest about what's synthetic. Feeds, events, quotes, severity: real. The book: synthetic, until the pilot.
